1. Goal and design

One targets pipeline that produces the thermal-response estimates under several methodological variants side by side — the original logic from site_info.csv, the choices recommended by the soil-temperature analysis, and variations of those — and compares them in a Quarto report, with every number traceable to the choice that produced it. It has to be modular enough that a new option (ERA5-Land soil temperature, a different growing-season rule) is one row in a registry rather than a fork of the code.

The recipe

A recipe is a named set of choices, one strategy per axis. Sites × recipes is the pipeline's grid.

What each strategy does — one bullet per cell of the Strategies column; parenthesised names are not built yet

Every strategy is a branch of the matching choose_*() in R/strategies.R, and every branch only selects: step 01 has already produced each candidate column and each bounds definition, so a strategy names one and records the reason next to the result. The full write-up is docs/recipes.md.

ts — which soil-temperature column the model is fitted on

The verdict these strategies branch on is one GOOD/BAD per site, not per timestamp, so the substitution is of the whole column at a site — never of the bad stretches within an otherwise good record. (TS_linear and TS_memfill are themselves hybrids at the row level: each writes its prediction over measured soil temperature only where a prediction exists, so measured values survive wherever a predictor is missing. That is overlay_ts(), and it is the manuscript's semantics.)

  • site_info — the ts_col declaration in site_info.csv. The manuscript's choice, site by site, as a person made it.
  • screen_best — TS_measured unless the in-pipeline quality verdict is BAD, in which case TS_linear, the TS ~ TA regression the manuscript would have used anyway. This isolates one change and nothing else: stop discarding sensors the data says are fine (F10 — 27 of 50 hand-flagged sites pass every physical test).
  • memory_fill — as screen_best, but a BAD sensor is replaced by TS_memfill, the reconstruction the per-site blocked-CV scorer picked (F12 — memory features cut out-of-fold error 58%). If the fill is unavailable the strategy falls back to TS_linear and writes why into settings$ts_reason, rather than failing the site.
  • (era5) — planned: ERA5-Land stl1/stl2 bias-corrected to the tower over the periods the screen passes. It needs a CDS download target and has to beat the 1.26 °C the memory features already reach.

The verdict is computed on the column step 01 leaves as TS_measured, i.e. after that step's site-specific reconstructions — which is why V4 can see a fill rediscover a regression.

season — the day-of-year span the 14-day windows tile

  • detect — the detected growing season. Today's behaviour, and the name understates it: detect_growing_season() derives gStart/gEnd from the day-of-year NEE climatology, raises gStart to the first day whose mean soil temperature is non-negative, and then overwrites either bound with the literal value in site_info.csv where one is declared. Twenty-four of the 117 rows declare at least one — including two of the six development sites, FI-Sod (gEnd = 270) and NL-Loo (gStart = 120) — so at those sites the "detected" season is partly a hand-set one. A force_detect strategy that ignores the overrides would separate the two and is listed in §6.
  • whole_year — a full year of DOY, starting where the site's growing year starts: 1–366 at an ordinary site and 183–548 at one whose growing year is wrapped, so the span is in the same coordinates as the data. Only the window layout changes; the detected season still drives the year gap scan and the control-year choice, because each needs a span to be defined over. Tests whether season detection is redundant with the fit-stage guards — V3 says it is not.
  • (satellite) — placeholder for a remotely-sensed phenology span in place of the flux-derived one.

bounds — which population the window-skip percentiles (tStart/tEnd) are taken from

  • native — each column's own definition, as the manuscript had it: the day-of-year climatology for the measured column, the raw half-hourly values for the regressed one. F4 measured the inconsistency this creates — the admissible band is 10.0 °C wide under one definition and 16.7 °C under the other, before the column changes at all.
  • climatology — the day-of-year-climatology definition, applied to whichever column the ts axis selected.
  • halfhourly — the half-hourly definition, likewise. Step 01 emits both rows for every column, so this is a lookup and not a recomputation.

The climatology row for TS_measured is not the native row: the native one is computed before disqualified years are dropped and is then floored, the consistent one on the step-01 output. Both sit in ts_bounds flagged by native, so a recipe cannot pick one thinking it is the other.

swc — which soil-water column the direct model uses

  • site_info — measured where SWC_use = YES, ERA5-Land otherwise. Today's behaviour.
  • era5 — ERA5-Land everywhere, so the soil-water driver is one product across sites. The total model has no soil water in its formula, so the choice is NA there whatever the recipe says.

year_qc — how years are qualified prep stage

  • site_info — the gap scan plus the hand-maintained year_removed list. The only strategy built.
  • (computed) — planned: a scored rule (gap statistics, u* coverage, residual anomalies) replacing the manual list, validated against the 20 sites that carry one. Being a prep-stage axis, it is the first strategy that changes the prep key and so the first that needs site_data mapped over crossing(site, prep_key); _targets.R asserts this and its message names the spot.

The stage column is the efficiency design. Step 01 (prep_nee_ac()) costs 30–140 s a site and step 02 costs hundreds of Stan fits; every axis whose choice can be deferred to step 02 is, so that recipes differing only in a fit choice share one step-01 result. Step 01 therefore produces every candidate soil-temperature column and both bounds definitions for each, and a recipe selects. This is the pattern docs/derived-columns.md already established for TS_measured/TS_linear, generalised.

A strategy is a choice on one axis; a recipe is a full set of them and is what a run is made under. The registry is data-core/recipes.csv, one row each, and these are the names used throughout §4 and §5. original is the oracle; every other row differs from it in as few axes as possible, so that a difference in the results has one cause.

3. Progress log

Phase 0 — merge and branch Done

  • fix-refactor-blockers merged into ts-rework at 10a4a80. The two branches touched no file in common, and the merge was clean. It brings in R/write-outputs.R, the tar_combine collectors and CSV writers, a tar_quarto run report (reports/pipeline-report.qmd), download targets for WorldClim/GSOC/BADM, and pinned quarto/r-insight.
  • Branch ts-variants created from the merge, same worktree.

Phase 1 — recipe machinery Done; oracle identical

What a recipe is in code. A validated S3 list (R/recipes.R), not an S7 class: recipes travel through targets' qs2 store and through tar_map() values, and an S7 object does not come back identical() from qs2 while a classed list does. Validation runs at construction against RECIPE_AXES, so an unknown strategy fails by name at pipeline definition, not inside the one target that reaches it. read_recipes() also refuses a registry whose original row disagrees with original_recipe() in code — the oracle comparison has to be against the right thing.

Where the choices are made. R/strategies.R has one choose_*() per axis, each a switch() whose branches name a column, a span or a bounds row. None computes anything; step 01 produces every candidate and these select. Every function returns the reason for its choice alongside it, and total_tas_site() writes those reasons into settings, so provenance is a column of the results rather than a reconstruction after the fact.

Step 01 now produces both bounds definitions for every column. The ts_bounds table gained definition and native columns. The native rows are the manuscript's numbers exactly — the measured column's carries the 0 °C floor and, at three sites, the 2 °C truncation, so it is written directly rather than by the generic ts_bounds_rows() — and ts_bounds_for(ts_bounds, ts_col) with no definition still returns the native row, which is what every pre-existing caller means. It also produces ts_qc, the quality verdict on TS_measured that the screen_best and memory_fill strategies branch on.

Backward compatibility, checked. total_tas_site() with no recipe uses original_recipe(); on DE-RuC the structure-only output with recipe = NULL and with recipe = original_recipe() is identical(). The unit suite still passes (389) and tests/ts-swc-baseline.R is unchanged — its call signature was preserved on purpose.

Fit fidelity. fit_settings("full") is the manuscript's sampler configuration; "fast" is two chains, a few hundred iterations, no retry. Threaded as an argument and baked into each fit target's command via the grid, so changing THERMAL_FIT invalidates exactly the fits and a store cannot mix profiles silently; settings$fit_profile records it and the report banners it.

Phase 2 — per-site inputs Done

R/ts-qc.R (diagnostics + verdict) and R/ts-fill.R (methods, blockings, scorer, fill_soil_temp()) are the promoted scripts/ code; the scripts now source them from R/ so there is one definition of each. fill_soil_temp() is a per-site, recipe-independent target: it scores every method out of fold under leave-one-year-out, picks the lowest RMSE, refits on all measured rows, predicts everywhere, overlays measured where a predictor is missing (the TS_linear semantics), and returns the column aligned to both step-01 tables plus its bounds rows and the CV table. It never errors — a site nothing can be fitted at returns a status, and the strategy falls back and says so — because under error = "continue" an error there would take every recipe at the site down with it.

On DE-RuC: verdict GOOD (amplitude ratio 0.50, lag 1.4 h); rf_memory wins the CV at 1.20 °C against lm_ta_pos's 2.61; 1.35 min.

Phase 3 — the grid Done

Two tar_map()s. The per-site one produces site_info, site_dl, site_data, site_fill once each. The fit one maps over crossing(site, recipe, model) and references its site's inputs as symbols built from the site name — the device write_respiration_all() already used — so a fit depends on exactly its own site. Recipes are targets read from the registry file, so editing a row invalidates exactly the fits made under it. Everything is combined once over the whole grid with recipe_id and model as columns; the manuscript-layout CSVs the workflows/ scripts read are the original slice of those tables under their old names, so nothing downstream changes.

_targets.R asserts that every recipe in a run shares one prep key. All five do today; the first recipe that does not (a computed year qualification) needs site_data mapped over crossing(site, prep_key), and the assertion's message says so. Under the dev grid: 119 targets, 60 fits.

4. Findings

V1 At DE-RuC the guards do not remove the out-of-season windows: noseason fits 272 cells against 88

Structural run (fit = FALSE, exact). Under original the detected season DOY 83–194 gives 8 windows and 88 (window × year) cells. Under noseason the whole year gives 26 windows and 272 cells, with one window skipped. The hypothesis that the fit-stage guards would discard what season detection discards predicts roughly 88 either way. DE-RuC is a temperate oceanic (Cfb) site whose soil rarely drops below the 2 °C floor, so this is the case the hypothesis was weakest for; the report's per-site table of fitted cells outside the detected season is the general answer, and boreal sites will look different.

At the two boreal sites the hypothesis holds exactly: the 2 °C floor and the observation-count guard remove every out-of-season window, and nothing that season detection would have excluded survives. At the four temperate sites it fails, by an amount that tracks how mild the winter is — DE-RuC's soil almost never reaches the floor, and 64% of what it fits is out of season. Across the six: 33%.

Two further things the table shows. Even at the boreal sites noseason fits more cells (+25%), because a whole-year tiling has a different phase and its windows straddle the season's edges differently, so "the same windows" is not quite what survives even where the share outside is zero. And the recipe changes TAS even under the smoke-test profile — FI-Sod total −0.035 → −0.0005, NL-Loo 0.004 → 0.018 — which is direction, not size, until full has run. The redundancy claim is therefore true as a statement about cold sites and false as a statement about the method; a season-free pipeline would need the year-qualification rule in §6 and would still fit different windows.

V4 The recipes cannot see past step 01's hard-coded reconstructions — DE-Hte shows the limit exactly

Yes: the column named TS_measured at DE-Hte contains no measured soil temperature at all. The site is declared estimate_Ts = YES with estimate_ts_method = "linear regression", so step 01 calls fix_soil_temp(), which fits lm(TS ~ TA + NETRAD) on the hours where a sensor reading exists and then replaces the whole column with that model's prediction — every row, including the hours that were measured. (It trains on TS_F_MDS_2, the second depth, because DE-Hte's first layer is too sparse; and it predicts from gap-filled air temperature and net radiation.) What every later stage sees under the name TS_measured is therefore a pure two-predictor regression.

Three things follow, in order:

  1. The quality screen passes it — amplitude ratio 0.48, lag 0.86 h. The screen's job is to catch a soil column that is really air temperature, and a regression carrying net radiation is not air-like: the extra predictor supplies enough damping and phase lag to look like soil. So screened and memfill both select this column, and every recipe but noseason produces the same fit as original.
  2. The reconstruction then scores perfectly against it. fill_soil_temp() cross-validates its candidates against TS_measured as truth. One candidate, lm_ta_netrad, has exactly the functional form that made the column, so any subset of rows recovers the same coefficients: out-of-fold RMSE 1.5×10−14, machine precision. The fill has rediscovered its own truth. Arithmetically correct, and empty as a statement about soil.
  3. The variants cannot reach past it. Every strategy operates on the column step 01 hands over, downstream of that replacement, so at the 16 estimate_Ts sites (and CZ-Stn, FI-Sod, GF-Guy, whose columns are swapped or rebuilt by other hard-coded branches) changing the ts axis changes nothing that matters.

What is recorded about it. The fill now carries degenerate = TRUE when its winner's RMSE is indistinguishable from zero, and truth_synthetic = TRUE when the site is one at which step 01's column is a reconstruction by any declared mechanism — the second is needed because DE-Akm's random-forest column is reproduced by rf_ta_netrad to 0.30 °C rather than to zero. Every fit's settings row carries ts_measured_synthetic whatever the recipe, and the provenance table in the report shows all three. Undoing the reconstructions themselves is the data-driven column-choice item in §6, and it is a prep-stage axis.

V5 At two temperate sites, half the cells noseason fits lie entirely outside the detected season

From the rendered report: across DE-RuC and DE-Hte, 52% of the (window × year) cells the noseason recipe fitted lie wholly outside the detected growing season — 173 of 272 at DE-RuC (64%), fewer at DE-Hte, where the 2 °C floor skipped 15 of 26 windows. The hypothesis that the fit-stage guards would discard what season detection discards predicts a number near zero. What survives is what the guards were never designed to catch: shoulder-season windows with soil above 2 °C and positive nighttime respiration. Both sites are temperate (Cfb, Dfb); the six-site run adds FI-Sod and SE-Deg, where winter soil sits below the floor for months and the share should fall. Whatever it is per climate, the report now computes it per site.

The TAS consequences are visible even under fast: DE-Hte 0.113 → 0.081 with the p-value rising from 0.003 to 0.012; DE-RuC 0.023 → 0.013. These are smoke-test numbers — two chains, 400 iterations — and are reported as direction only.

V6 DE-RuC under measured soil temperature (fast profile, direction only)

DE-RuC declares TS_linear. Under original its TAS is 0.023 (p = 0.057); under every recipe that selects the measured column — screened, memfill, memfill_hh — it is 0.007 (p ≈ 0.68), with the control year moving 2024 → 2020 (V2). The three measured-column recipes agree with each other to 6×10−4, which is about the sampler noise under this profile, as they should: they differ only in the bounds definition, and at DE-RuC the wider half-hourly band skips no additional window. The direction — a 70% drop when the regression is replaced by the sensor — is the same as F8 found at the site where TAS was significant; the size is not to be read until the full profile has run.

V2 Selecting measured soil temperature changes the control year

DE-RuC declares TS_linear. Under original its control year is 2024; under every recipe that selects TS_measured it is 2020. The control year sets ERref_control, the denominator of every lnRatio the TAS regression is fitted to — F3 in ts-rework.html found this at 37 of 43 sites and here it is again in the pipeline itself, now recorded in settings$control_year per recipe.

V3 The consistent climatology bounds are not the native ones

DE-RuC, measured column: native [5.16, 17.29], consistent climatology [5.38, 17.17], half-hourly [3.50, 20.90]. The native row is computed by detect_growing_season() on the record before disqualified years are dropped and then floored; the consistent row is computed on the step-01 output. Close, not equal, and both are in ts_bounds flagged by native, so a recipe cannot pick one thinking it is the other.

What fast is and is not. fit_settings("fast") is two chains and a few hundred iterations with no divergence retry — roughly a fifth of the sampler work of full, which is the manuscript's configuration. It exists to prove that every recipe, collector and report runs end to end. Its TAS values are smoke-test artefacts: settings$fit_profile records the profile on every row and both reports put a banner at the top when they see it.

On YCRC. submit.sh exports the full-grid configuration and runs pixi run targets; it is only the controller job. _targets.R selects crew_controller_slurm() when the hostname matches ycrc.yale.edu, and that launches 20 worker jobs of 12 h each with four tasks apiece. Two things to check before submitting:

  • data-raw/ has the flux products for the 44 handled sites (the FLUXNET-family, northern-hemisphere set; AmeriFlux BASE sites are outside pipeline_sites()). download_site() fetches missing ones, but the ICOS and FLUXNET downloads need the credentials in _creds.toml.
  • The 12.5 h controller limit in submit.sh is for the controller; worker time is in _targets.R. If the run is cut off, re-submitting resumes: targets skips everything already built.

What "ran to completion" looks like. reports/variant-comparison.html exists and its metadata block lists 44 sites, 5 recipes, both models and profile full; the oracle section reports how many of the original site-model pairs agree with data-proc-original/ to within the noise floor; data-proc/analysis/variant_outcome.csv has one row per (site, recipe, model) that fitted. Under error = "continue" a site that fails does so alone — targets::tar_meta(fields = error) lists them — and every other target still builds.

What to expect from the numbers. Some recipes will fail at some sites by construction rather than by bug: a memory_fill site whose fill could not be scored falls back to TS_linear and says so in ts_reason; a site whose measured column the screen fails under screened gets the regression the manuscript used anyway. The comparison report's provenance table is where to read those.

6. Documented future work

Written up in full in docs/recipes.md; the summary here is so the shape of each item is visible without opening the repo.

Asked for A fully season-free variant: what the year-qualification rule needs

noseason deliberately changes only the window layout. Making the pipeline independent of season detection altogether means replacing two more uses of gStart/gEnd:

  1. Year qualification (get_good_years()). Gaps are counted within the season and the thresholds scale with its length — max(31, 0.225 × length) days for the largest gap, max(1/3, …) for the total. Applied to a whole year this would reject almost every boreal year, where a winter without nighttime NEE is normal. A season-free rule has to say what part of the year a site is expected to have data in without detecting a season. Two candidates: qualify a year on the fraction of the site's typical covered days-of-year it covers (a per-site data-density profile); or qualify per window rather than per year and let the fit-stage nobs guard do the rest.
  2. Control year (total_tas_site()): the year whose growing-season mean soil temperature is closest to the long-term mean. The natural season-free replacement is the year whose mean over the fitted windows is closest to the mean.

Item 1 is a step-01 change and so a new year_qc strategy — the first recipe with a different prep key, and therefore the first that needs site_data mapped over crossing(site, prep_key). The assertion in _targets.R names the spot. Item 2 is a step-02 change and slots into choose_window_season()'s neighbour.
