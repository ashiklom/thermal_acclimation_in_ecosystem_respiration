The soil-temperature sensor screen

Is a soil-temperature record physically plausible as buried soil temperature, and which parts of it are not? This page documents the screen as implemented in R/ts-screen.R; it reads no pipeline results. The thresholds quoted are read from the R constants, the R snippets are extracted from the code at build time, and the real-data examples are the screen (ts_screen_series()) run on the raw DE-Hai record by scripts/make-docs-examples.R (with a fixed June–August growing season). The simulations on this page re-implement the screen's rules in JavaScript, with the same constants.

The screen depends only on the input data (step 01's record and the raw sensor), never on a fit. For each site it screens four series: the sensor as recorded (TS_original, where usable as step 01 would use it), its real readings (TS_real: measured, not gap-filled), step 01's column (TS_reconstructed) and the manuscript's (TS_manuscript). Each gets a verdict (GOOD, SUSPECT, BAD), flags, yearly diagnostics and a table of bad time windows for a later reconstruction step.

The heat-conduction basis

Heat conduction damps and delays the daily cycle of air temperature with depth z. For a sinusoidal surface temperature in a uniform soil (thermal diffusivity κ), the daily cycle at depth z has its range reduced and its peak delayed by

rangerangeair= e−z/d, lag=zd·242π h, d=2κ/ω ≈10 cm

(d: the damping depth of the daily cycle, ω = 2π / 24 h.) Both observables imply a depth in damping depths, and in a conducting soil the two agree:

ẑamp=−ln(range ratio), ẑlag=lag·2π24, ẑlag=ẑamp for conduction

So a sensor that reports air temperature (or sits at the surface) has a range ratio near 1 and no lag; one whose peak comes before air's, one without a daily cycle, or one that repeats values is not measuring buried soil.

Peak hour and lag

The peak hour of a mean diurnal cycle v(h) is taken from the phase of its first harmonic, which, unlike the hour of the maximum, varies continuously (with values at fewer than 12 times of day it is missing):

hpeak=242π atan2( ∑hv(h)sinθh, ∑hv(h)cosθh )mod24, θh=2πh/24

The lag is the difference of the series' and air's peak hours, wrapped into [−12, 12) h. Phase is known only modulo 24 h, so the lag is then unwrapped with the lag the damping implies: of the lags that differ by whole days, the one closest to ẑamp · 24 / 2π is kept:

lag=w+24·round( ẑamp·24/2π−w24), w=((hpeaksoil− hpeakair+12)mod24)−12

The demo shows why the harmonic is used: a daily cycle with a sharp afternoon peak (a second harmonic) and noise. The hour of the maximum jumps between half-hours as the noise changes; the first-harmonic peak moves smoothly.

The diurnal diagnostics

Over the growing season only (gStart … gEnd of step 01: the period TAS is estimated over, free of snow cover), the screen computes from the series and air temperature (ts_diurnal_diagnostics()):

  • each day's range (max − min) of each series, on days where the series has at least max(4, ⌊0.8 × records per day⌋) values (38 of 48 half-hours); the days where both have a range (at least 5, else everything is missing);
  • amp_ratio = median daily range of the series / median daily range of air temperature, over those common days;
  • lag_h: the peak hours of the mean diurnal cycles (mean by time of day over the season), their wrapped difference, unwrapped with the damping as above;
  • zd_amp = −ln(amp_ratio) and zd_lag = lag_h · 2π / 24, the two implied depths.

Whole-record diagnostics beside them: coverage (share of records with a value), mean_offset (growing-season mean of series − air), stuck_frac and stuck_max_h (below) and spike_rate (share of steps larger than 2 °C per hour of time step, i.e. 1 °C per half hour; reported, not used by any rule).

The range-ratio / lag plane

Each series is a point in the plane of its range ratio and lag. The whole-record rules carve out regions: air-like (range ratio above and |lag| below h), flat (range ratio below ) and leads air (lag below h). Conduction puts a sensor on the curve lag = −ln(ratio) · 24 / 2π; the non-conductive check (SUSPECT) fires where the series is damped (ẑamp > 0.05) but the two implied depths differ by more than a factor of or the lag is not positive: outside the green band. A regression on air temperature, for example, is damped but has no lag.

Stuck runs and the zero curtain

A run is a stretch of exactly repeated consecutive values (a missing value breaks a run). Runs whose value is within ±0.5 °C of 0 °C are exempt: under snow, freezing or thawing soil sits at 0 °C for days (the zero curtain), which is physics, not a stuck logger (ts_runs()). Two rules use the runs:

  • stuck (BAD, whole record): the share of the series' values in runs of at least 6 records (3 h at half-hourly records) exceeds ; runs anywhere in the record count, also under snow away from 0 °C (only the stuck-period windows below are restricted to the growing season), so a long snow cover with the soil steady a little above or below the zero band can by itself make a series BAD as stuck.
  • stuck_periods (SUSPECT, a bad window): a run of at least h that lies mostly (more than half of its records) in the growing season becomes a bad window of scale "period"; runs less than h apart merge into one window. Under snow, soil temperature can also sit at one logger reading for days away from 0 °C (in the first network screen most stuck-period windows were in November–March), so long runs outside the growing season are not windows.

The yearly screen: changepoints and odd years

Every growing year is screened separately: its growing-season coverage, mean offset from air temperature and its own diurnal diagnostics (range ratio, lag, number of days with a range). A year is screenable if offset, log range ratio and lag are all finite and it has at least 20 days with a range. Three yearly metrics are compared between years: offset (°C), log_amp = ln(range ratio) (the depth in damping depths) and lag_h (h).

Changepoints

A changepoint is a lasting shift of the diagnostics (a sensor moved or replaced). With at least screenable years, every split into an earlier segment a and a later segment b (at least years each) is scored for each metric:

D=med(b)−med(a), S=1.4826·med(|a−med(a)|∪|b−med(b)|), score=|D|max(S,10−9)

A metric agrees if its score exceeds and |D| exceeds its minimum shift (offset °C, log range ratio , lag h). A split counts if at least metrics agree: the range ratio and lag alone vary between years with soil moisture (the damping depth does), while a moved or replaced sensor shifts several together. The counting split with the largest summed score of its agreeing metrics is the changepoint; binary segmentation then searches each of the two segments it leaves (each again needing years), and so on. Everything before the latest changepoint becomes one bad window: by convention the latest segment, the current sensor set-up, is the reference, as physics alone cannot tell which side is wrong.

Odd years

Within each segment with at least 3 screenable years, a year is odd under a metric if its deviation from the segment's median exceeds the metric's minimum shift and either the segment's spread is zero or the deviation exceeds spreads; a year is flagged (shift_<metric>) if at least metrics are odd. Runs of consecutive flagged years become bad windows of scale "year". The spread here is 1.4826 * stats::mad(); since mad() already scales by 1.4826, it is 1.4826² ≈ 2.2 times the median absolute deviation, a stricter threshold than the changepoint score's.

Verdicts and flags

A series is BAD if any whole-record rule fires (these rules and thresholds follow the alexey branch's screen, calibrated there against the project's hand-made calls); else SUSPECT if only the newer checks fire (the heat-equation consistency check, or a part of the record is bad: a window); else GOOD. flags lists every rule that fired, BAD rules first; bad_fraction is the share of the series' records inside bad windows.

The R screen of DE-Hai's five sensors (raw record, June–August as the growing season): the shallowest sensor is SUSPECT, non-conductive, because its lag is shorter than its damping implies (a near-surface sensor is heated by radiation at the soil surface, which peaks before air temperature does, so it lags air less than conduction from air would predict); the two deepest sensors have almost no daily cycle and are BAD as flat, the deepest also as stuck (coarse readings of a slowly changing temperature). The screen asks whether a series behaves like soil temperature near the surface, where respiration happens; it does not judge whether a deep sensor works.

Bad windows and ts_screen_mask()

The bad windows are machine-readable (ts_screen_windows.csv; time stamps "YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS" on the record's own clock, start and end inclusive), one row per window and series:

columnmeaning
site_ID, columnthe site and the series screened
start, endfirst and last time stamp of the window (inclusive)
scalechangepoint (all growing years before the latest changepoint, whole years), year (a run of consecutive odd growing years) or period (stuck runs)
rulechangepoint_<metric> for every metric of every changepoint; shift_<metric> for odd years; stuck
valuechangepoint: the earlier segment's median of the latest change's strongest metric; year: the flagged years' median of their first metric; period: total length of the merged runs (h)
referencechangepoint: the later segment's median; year: the segment's median of that metric; period: TS_SCREEN_STUCK_PERIOD_H

The manuscript's corrections: a guideline, not a target

  
ts_screen_validation() sets the screen beside what the manuscript did by hand, one row per site: the
    screen's verdict, flags and windows for the sensor (its real readings where they have a range ratio, else the sensor as
    recorded), and the share of the sensor's records that step 01's column and the fits' column replace (differ by more
    than 0.01 °C or are missing), the site's regression on air temperature, its sensor first-year cut-off and its removed
    years. The manuscript "acted" on a site if its fits replace more than 5% of the sensor, it set a first year, or it
    removed years. The comparison is a guideline, not a target: automated physical checks and hand-made calls can
    legitimately disagree, so the screen is not tuned to reproduce the manuscript's choices. (One systematic difference
    is by design: where the manuscript's fits used its regression on air temperature, TS_manuscript is damped
    but not delayed, exactly what the non-conductive check flags.)
