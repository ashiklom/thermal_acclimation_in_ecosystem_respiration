AU-Tum: which flux record for the latest data?

AU-Tum (Tumbarumba, a 40 m wet sclerophyll forest measured from a 70 m tower) is the one TERN site. Its published record is FLUXNET2015 (2001–2014). Two later records exist: the TERN product the FLUXNET-archive shuttle serves (TERN's own ONEFlux release 2026_r1, 2002–2022, with only constant-u* NEE), and TERN's L6 product (PyFluxPro, 2002–2026). This page compares the streams and shows AU-Tum's TAS under each candidate record, to choose how to extend AU-Tum. Data: scripts/compare-au-tum-sources.py and scripts/au-tum-source-options.R; TERN's variables as FLUXNET columns: scripts/download-tern.py.

How the streams are processed

The pipeline uses only night-time NEE observations: step 01 keeps hours with air temperature, NEE quality flag 0 (measured, passed the u* filter), NIGHT, and −5 < NEE < 30 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹. So what matters is which hours each stream keeps and what flux it reports for them, not its gap filling.

What differs in the CO₂ flux

ONEFlux builds NEE from what a network supplies, in this order (set_nee(), oneflux_steps/qc_auto): the network's NEE; else its CO₂ flux plus its storage flux; else its CO₂ flux plus a single-point storage term from the CO₂ concentration at the top of the tower, ΔCO₂/Δt × height / 0.024; else the CO₂ flux alone, only where the site declares storage negligible. The left figure computes that single-point term from each ONEFlux stream's own tower-top CO₂ and plots each stream's night NEE minus TERN L6's against it. The shuttle follows the 1:1 line: it is TERN's flux plus ONEFlux's single-point storage. FLUXNET2015 does not: its excess over TERN (+1 to +4 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹) is unrelated to the tower-top term. The right figure shows why: by day, when storage is small, FLUXNET2015's CO₂ flux is larger in magnitude than TERN's by a factor of 1.2–1.8 that changes from year to year. The heat fluxes, u* and radiation do not differ at all.

Findings

  1. TERN has reprocessed AU-Tum's CO₂ flux since FLUXNET2015. For the same hours, the newer records' CO₂ exchange is smaller in magnitude by day and by night. The daytime slope against FLUXNET2015 is 0.55–0.84 for TERN L6 and 0.57–0.91 for the shuttle, varying by year; night-time respiration is lower by 2.3 (TERN) and 1.0 (shuttle) µmol m⁻² s⁻¹, against a FLUXNET2015 night mean of ~7. Sensible and latent heat, u* and net radiation are identical in both, so only the CO₂ flux changed. Because the factor varies from year to year, at least one of the two records carries year-dependent CO₂ corrections that the other lacks. Which one is right cannot be told from the products; neither documents the change.
  2. Storage explains the shuttle–TERN difference, not the FLUXNET2015 one. The shuttle is TERN's CO₂ flux plus ONEFlux's tower-top storage term (r = 0.77). FLUXNET2015's excess over TERN does not follow that term (r = 0.12). It does grow on calm nights (3–4 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ at u* < 0.4 m s⁻¹, 0.5–1.2 above 0.6), as a storage term from a CO₂ profile through the canopy would; but how much of the night-time gap is storage and how much the general scaling cannot be separated here.
  3. The shuttle and TERN L6 otherwise agree: the same CO₂ flux (daytime slope 0.94), differing mainly in which night hours they keep. The shuttle's constant-u* filter is strict (median threshold 0.31 m s⁻¹ against 0.17 in FLUXNET2015; ~40% fewer night records). TERN's filter is lenient through 2019 (hours down to u* ≈ 0.01–0.05 m s⁻¹, which without storage underestimate respiration) and strict from 2020.
  4. Air temperature, soil temperature and soil water agree between FLUXNET2015 and both newer streams over 2002–2014 (hourly r ≥ 0.98, soil water against TERN 0.977). The exception is the shuttle's 2020–2021 soil data, which are constant fill values.
  5. The temperature sensitivity hardly changes; the base level does. Yearly Q10 from an exponential fit of night NEE on soil temperature varies alike in all streams over 2002–2014 (1.1–2.7), TERN's ~0.15 lower on average: a scaling of the flux changes the base level, not the temperature sensitivity. R10 is 5.0–7.2 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ in FLUXNET2015 against 3.9–5.6 (TERN) and 3.2–4.8 (shuttle) in the same years, and the mean night NEE 6.4–8.1 against 4.6–6.8 and 3.7–5.4. A record that switches from FLUXNET2015 to TERN therefore steps down by about a quarter in night-time respiration at the switch, whatever the temperature.
  6. The newer data add little after 2014: 2015–2019 (~370–670 night records a year), then 2021–2022 and 2025 with ~150–240. There is almost no flux in 2020 and none in 2023–2024.

Recommendation

For now, keep AU-Tum on FLUXNET2015 alone (no extension), and ask TERN which CO₂ processing is right. Splicing FLUXNET2015 with any of the newer records joins two different processings of the CO₂ flux. The difference is not only the storage term, as this page first concluded, but a year-varying scaling of the flux (0.55–0.84 by day). That makes a step of about a quarter in night-time respiration at the switch. It is undocumented, so it cannot be corrected with any confidence.

If AU-Tum must be extended, the options rank as follows:

  • FLUXNET2015 → TERN (FLUXNET2015+TERN) keeps the published years unchanged and adds the growing years 2014–2019, 2021 and 2025 (2014 straddles the switch; 8,946 night records instead of 5,413; 19 years instead of 11). Its TAS stays within one standard error of the published one and keeps its class: total −0.022 → −0.018, direct −0.018 → −0.008, both not significant. This is the least disruptive, but it carries the step above.
  • TERN alone (or the shuttle alone) is one consistent processing of the CO₂ flux, but it replaces the published years and changes AU-Tum's answer: the direct TAS moves from −0.018 (n.s.) to +0.032 (significantly positive), about 3 standard errors; the total TAS from −0.022 to +0.019. That would be the right choice only if TERN confirms that its current CO₂ processing supersedes FLUXNET2015's. TERN L6 also keeps low-turbulence nights that the shuttle discards.
  • The shuttle between FLUXNET2015 and TERN adds nothing TERN lacks for 2015–2022: the same CO₂ flux plus ONEFlux's tower-top storage, with a third u* method (CUT only, the strictest). Its soil temperature and soil water for 2020–2021 are constant fill values, which step 01 would use for soil water. Not fitted, for these reasons.

Open questions for TERN (support@fluxnet.org, or the site team at CSIRO): what changed in AU-Tum's CO₂ flux processing since the data supplied to FLUXNET2015, and why the change is year-dependent; whether the FLUXNET2015 input included a storage term from a CO₂ profile; and why the 2026_r1 ONEFlux run produced no VUT although the moving-point u* method succeeded in every year. Also note: in the extended options the control year becomes 2025, a sparse TERN year (180 night records), because it is the year closest to the mean growing-season soil temperature.
