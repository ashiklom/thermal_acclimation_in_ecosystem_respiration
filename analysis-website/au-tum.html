AU-Tum: which flux record for the latest data?

AU-Tum (Tumbarumba, a 40 m wet sclerophyll forest measured from a 70 m tower) is the one TERN site. Its published record is FLUXNET2015 (2001–2014). Two later records exist: the FLUXNET-archive shuttle's TERN product (ONEFlux v1.3, 2002–2022, but only constant-u* NEE), and TERN's own L6 product (PyFluxPro, 2002–2026). This page compares the streams and shows AU-Tum's TAS under each candidate record, to choose how to extend AU-Tum. Data: scripts/compare-au-tum-sources.py and scripts/au-tum-source-options.R; TERN's variables as FLUXNET columns: scripts/download-tern.py.

How the streams are processed

The pipeline uses only night-time NEE observations: step 01 keeps hours with air temperature, NEE quality flag 0 (measured, passed the u* filter), NIGHT, and −5 < NEE < 30 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹. So what matters is which hours each stream keeps and what flux it reports for them, not its gap filling.

Findings

  1. The newer records report less night-time respiration than FLUXNET2015 for the same hours: by 2.3 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ on average for TERN L6 and 1.0 for the shuttle, against a FLUXNET2015 night mean of ~7. The gap grows as turbulence falls: 3–4 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ at u* < 0.4 m s⁻¹, 0.5–1.2 above 0.6. That is the signature of the CO₂ storage term, large in still nights under a tall canopy. FLUXNET2015 evidently includes it. TERN's current data, and the shuttle's ONEFlux run on it, have none before 2020.
  2. The shuttle and TERN L6 agree: both come from TERN's current data. For the hours both select they differ by 0.24 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ (r = 0.96). They differ mainly in which hours they keep. The shuttle's constant-u* filter is strict (u* ≥ ~0.35 m s⁻¹, ~40% fewer night records). TERN's filter is lenient through 2019 (hours down to u* ≈ 0.01–0.05 m s⁻¹, which without storage underestimate respiration) and strict from 2020.
  3. Air temperature, soil temperature and soil water agree between FLUXNET2015 and both newer streams over 2002–2014 (hourly r ≥ 0.98, soil water against TERN 0.977). The exception is the shuttle's 2020–2021 soil data, which are constant fill values.
  4. The temperature sensitivity hardly changes; the base level does. Yearly Q10 from an exponential fit of night NEE on soil temperature varies alike in all streams over 2002–2014 (1.1–2.7), TERN's ~0.15 lower on average. R10 is 5.0–7.2 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ in FLUXNET2015 against 3.9–5.6 (TERN) and 3.2–4.8 (shuttle) in the same years, and the mean night NEE 6.4–8.1 against 4.6–6.8 and 3.7–5.4. A record that switches from FLUXNET2015 to TERN therefore steps down by about a quarter in night-time respiration at the switch, whatever the temperature.
  5. The newer data add little after 2014: 2015–2019 (~370–670 night records a year), then 2021–2022 and 2025 with ~150–240. There is almost no flux in 2020 and none in 2023–2024.
