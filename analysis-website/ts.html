Soil-temperature columns

Every site gets every candidate soil temperature: the sensor as recorded (TS_original), step 01's repaired or reconstructed column (TS_reconstructed), what the manuscript's fits used (TS_manuscript: the regression on air temperature at 35 sites), TS_linear (TS ~ TA above freezing, fitted to the sensor) and other estimators trained only on real sensor data. Each column is qualified as step 01 qualifies its own: its growing-season start, temperature range, years kept and nighttime fit records. Estimators are scored against the sensor out of fold (each year predicted by a model trained on the others) and for plausibility against air temperature.

Where each estimator is best or close to the top 3

Number of sites at which the estimator is best, or within 10% of the third best (r²: 0.01), under each metric. An estimator that is "top" anywhere is carried forward to the fits.

Sensor screen

Is each site's soil-temperature sensor behaving like buried soil temperature? Heat conduction damps and delays the daily cycle of air temperature with depth, so over the growing season a buried sensor's daily range is a fraction of air's and its daily peak comes later; a sensor reporting air (range ratio near 1, no lag), leading air, flat, stuck or sparse is not measuring soil. BAD: one of those whole-record rules fires. SUSPECT: only the newer checks fire: damping and delay imply different depths (non-conductive; e.g. a regression on air temperature is damped but not delayed), the yearly diagnostics change for good (changepoint: the record before the latest segment is a bad window), single odd years, or stuck periods of a day or more. Bad windows are machine-readable (ts_screen_windows.csv) for a later reconstruction step. The screen uses only the input data. Beside it, what the manuscript did by hand: a guideline, not a target.
