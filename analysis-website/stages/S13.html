BREAKING. The direct model uses the daytime NEE of the day before each night (a 3-day running mean). It was joined by (year, DOY − 1 before noon), with three flaws: (1) every 1 January's pre-noon records took the same day's value (the rule exempted the first DOY of the record from the shift, which is DOY 1 every year); (2) at southern-hemisphere sites, whose DOY runs 183…548, the daily table was sorted by calendar year then wrapped DOY, so the running mean jumped across New Year; (3) the running mean ran over table rows, spanning gaps, and days without daytime data counted as 0.

Now the daily table is keyed by calendar date over the complete, sorted calendar of the record; records before noon take the previous date at every site and on every day; the 3-day mean is over the days with data (NA only if none of the three has any). Nighttime records without a value are dropped before both models, as before.

Result. The joined values change at every site, but little: the sites' mean daytime NEE moves by a median 0.003%. Nighttime records are gained only at the Arctic sites (US-ICh +405, US-ICs +260, US-ICt +255, FI-Sod +225), where records whose own day had no daylight now take the neighbouring days' mean, and 34 sites lose 1–17 records (days without daytime NEE are now missing rather than zero). Total TAS changes beyond the threshold only at BR-Sa1; direct TAS at five sites (largest BR-Sa1, +0.011). No significance class changes.
