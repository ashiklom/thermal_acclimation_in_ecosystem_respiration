Drivers of TAS and future respiration

What explains why acclimation differs between sites, and how much it matters for respiration in a warmer climate.

Drivers. Random forests relate each site's TAS to its elevation, mean annual air temperature, leaf area index, soil organic carbon and the warming rate of its air-temperature record. Variable importance is the increase in the forest's mean squared error when a predictor is permuted; partial dependence is the forest's prediction across one predictor's range with the others averaged out (median and 90 % band over bootstrap forests). Besides total and direct TAS, the apparent TAS (total − direct: the indirect effects) is analysed.

Future respiration. Each site's growing-season nighttime respiration is projected for 2041–2060 under SSP2-4.5 from its respiration–temperature model and the projected warming of minimum (nighttime) temperature (the mean of the climate models, carried from air to soil temperature by the site's soil–air temperature slope), once with the short-term temperature response alone and once also shifted by the site's total TAS: respiration × exp(TAS × warming).
