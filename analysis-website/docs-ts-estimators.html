Soil-temperature columns and estimators

How every site gets several candidate soil-temperature columns, how the estimators among them are trained and scored against the site's own sensor, and how the ones carried forward to the fits are chosen. This page documents the code (R/ts-columns.R, R/ts-ranking.R); it reads no pipeline results. The R snippets are extracted from the code at build time, the thresholds quoted are read from it, and the real-data examples come from the raw DE-Hai record (scripts/make-docs-examples.R).

The candidate columns

The manuscript used one soil temperature per site: the sensor, repaired or reconstructed in step 01 at some sites, and replaced in step 02 by a regression on air temperature at the regression sites. Here every site gets every candidate, each qualified the way step 01 qualifies its own (where it is usable, its growing-season start, temperature range, years kept and nighttime fit records), so that a fit can use any of them.

Air temperature, NEE, u* filtering and the NEE-uptake days are the same for every column (step 01's); what depends on the column is where it is usable, the growing-season start and temperature range, the years kept and the control year.

Why air temperature alone is a poor predictor of buried soil temperature

Soil is heated and cooled from the surface by conduction. For a periodic surface temperature, the heat equation in a uniform soil of thermal diffusivity κ has an exact solution: each period (the day, the year, a passing weather system) is damped exponentially with depth and delayed in proportion to depth, with a damping depth that grows with the period:

T(z,t)= T¯+ ∑k Ak e−z/dk sin(ωkt+φk −z/dk), dk= 2κ/ωk

With κ = 0.5 × 10⁻⁶ m² s⁻¹ the daily cycle's damping depth is about 12 cm and the annual cycle's about 2.2 m. At 10 cm the daily range of air temperature is cut to less than half and arrives hours late, while the seasonal cycle passes almost unchanged but lags by days. So soil temperature at a given air temperature depends on what air temperature has been: the soil is cooler than the same air temperature in spring and warmer in autumn (a hysteresis loop), and a regression on air temperature alone (TS_linear, the manuscript's step-02 regression) overstates the daily cycle, has no lag and mixes spring and autumn. Right: daily means of the year, soil against air, coloured by day of year; the line is lm(TS ~ TA) fitted to the hourly records with TA > 0 (the linear estimator). The estimators

Each estimator is a model kind, a set of predictors and the training records it uses; this table is generated from ts_estimators() itself. lm is ordinary least squares (stats::lm()); rf is a ranger random forest. Every estimator becomes a column TS_<name>.

Training data, gap filling and fitting

  • Trained only on real sensor readings (ts_truth()): the column of real readings (site_settings.csv ts_sensor_truth: the sensor itself, or the non-filled counterpart of a PI gap-filled one), where the source flags quality only the measured records (flag 0, not gap-filled), and only from ts_sensor_first_year on where that is set. A site without such readings has all-missing estimator columns.
  • Predictors gap-filled first (ts_gapfill_doy()): a missing air temperature or net radiation is replaced by its mean over all years at the same day of year and time of day (as the original 01_01 script did); values with no such mean stay missing, and so does the prediction there.
  • Too little training data: an estimator needs at least TS_MIN_TRAIN = complete training records (after its own subset, e.g. TA > 0), or its column is all missing.
  • Forests: 200 trees, one thread, trained on at most 60,000 records (a random subset, sorted back into time order) for the columns and 20,000 within the cross-validation; the seed is a hash of the site, the purpose and the estimator (tas_seed(site, "ts", name), and tas_seed(site, "ts-cv", name, year) per held-out year), so every run reproduces the same forests.
  • Prediction: every record with complete predictors, over the whole record (also where the sensor has readings: the column is the estimator, not a gap-filled sensor).

Memory features

The memory estimators give the model what the physics says soil temperature depends on: past air temperature over several time scales and the position in the seasonal and daily cycles. For a record at time of day h:m on calendar day d (air temperature gap-filled first; days on a complete calendar, so a day without data is a missing value, not skipped):

TAday(d) =mean of TA over the records of day d TAlag1(d) =TAday(d−1) TAamp_lag1(d) =maxTA−minTA on day d−1  (missing with fewer than 4 values) TAmk(d) =1|J| ∑j∈J TAday(d−j), J={j∈0…k−1 :TAday(d−j) present}, k∈{3,7,30} doy_sn,doy_cn =sin,cos(n·2π yday(d)/365.25), n∈{1,2} hr_s1,hr_c1 =sin,cos(2π(h+m/60)/24)

The running means are right-aligned (the day itself and the days before it; at the start of the record over the days there are). Note that TAday and the running means include the whole of the current day, so a record's features include air temperature later that day. Together with the record's own TA these are the 13 predictors of lm_memory, rf_memory and (with net radiation) rf_memory_netrad. The figure shows them for DE-Hai in 2014 against the shallowest sensor's daily mean: the 7- and 30-day means follow the soil's seasonal course much more closely than the daily air temperature does.

Leave-one-year-out cross-validation

Estimators are scored against the sensor out of fold: for every year with real readings, the estimator is trained on the other years (truth & YEAR != y) and predicts the held-out year; the error metrics are computed over all years' out-of-fold predictions together. A site needs real readings in at least two years, or its estimators' metrics are missing.

Why whole years and not a random subset of records? Soil temperature is strongly autocorrelated, and part of its deviation from what air temperature predicts persists for days to months (soil moisture, snow cover, litter, a sensor's slow drift). With a random split, almost every held-out record has its neighbours of the same hours and days in the training set; a flexible model that can match records by their features (a forest, or any local-averaging method) then "predicts" a held-out record from its neighbours, slow anomaly included, and looks far better than it will be on a year it has not seen. Holding out a whole year removes the neighbours. The simulation below shows this with two models fitted to daily means: least squares on memory features, and k-nearest-neighbour regression on the same features (standing in for the forest, which also averages the training records whose predictors are close). The "new years" bars are the true error, on independently simulated years the models never saw.

Predictors: daily mean air temperature, its 7- and 30-day running means, and the first annual harmonic. Simulated soil temperature: a 10-day exponential memory of air temperature plus a slow anomaly (an AR(1) process with the time scale and sd set above, unrelated to air temperature) plus 0.2 °C noise. The right panel shows one year's sensor and the nearest-neighbour model's out-of-fold predictions under both splits: under the random split the predictions follow the slow anomaly they cannot know from air temperature.

Error and plausibility metrics

Agreement with the sensor

For predictions pi and the sensor's real readings oi on the n records where both are present (out of fold for the estimators; as they are for TS_reconstructed and TS_manuscript), with errors ei = pi − oi (all missing with fewer than two pairs):

rmse=1n∑iei2 bias=1n∑iei r2=cor(p,o)2 sd_ratio=sd(p)/sd(o) cell_sd=meancsd(ei:i∈c), c = day of year × hour (cells with one record have no sd and are skipped) year_sd=sdy(e¯y), e¯y = mean error in year y (missing with one year)

r² is the squared correlation, not 1 − SSE/SST: a column with the right shape but the wrong level or scale still has a high r²; bias and sd ratio catch those. cell_sd measures the error that is not a systematic function of the season and time of day (which a column could share with the sensor's climatology); year_sd measures how stable the column's bias is from year to year.

Plausibility against air temperature

Measured exactly as the sensor screen measures a series (the same function, ts_series_diagnostics()), for every column including the sensor; diurnal metrics over the growing season (day of year gStart … gEnd of step 01):

metricdefinition
amp_ratiomedian daily range of the column / median daily range of air temperature, over the days both have (days with at least 80% of their records)
lag_hfirst-harmonic peak hour of the column's mean daily cycle minus air temperature's, unwrapped towards the lag the range ratio implies for heat conduction
offsetmean of column − air temperature over the growing season
stuckshare of the column's values in runs of ≥ 6 repeated consecutive values; runs within 0.5 °C of 0 °C (the zero curtain under snow) exempt
spikesshare of steps between consecutive records of more than 2 °C per hour
d_amp, d_lag, d_offsetdistance from the sensor's own value (TS_original): |ln(amp_ratio / amp_ratiosensor)|, |wrap(lag_h − lag_hsensor)| (circular, −12…12 h: a nearly flat series can unwrap a day away), |offset − offsetsensor|; missing at sites without a sensor

Ranking and the "top" margin

At every site, the estimators (the TS_<name> columns of ts_estimators(), including TS_linear; not TS_original, TS_reconstructed or TS_manuscript) are ranked under each metric of TS_RANK_METRICS. Each metric is a score of the quality table, lower is better except r²:

With sj the score of estimator j (r²: sj = −r²j), over the estimators with a value:

rankj=1+#{i:si<sj}(ties share the lower rank) s(3)=the third smallest score (the largest, with fewer than three values) topj⇔rankj=1∨ sj≤s(3)+m, m={ for r² ·|s(3)|otherwise

"Best or within the margin of the third best": since the second and third best are at or below s(3), the three best always count as top, plus any estimator within the margin of the third. The selected set

The columns the fits are run with (ts_methods_selected) are the reference columns TS_REFERENCE_COLUMNS () and every estimator that is top at some site under some metric, in the order of ts_estimators(). Because the three best estimators are top at every site and metric, an estimator is dropped only if it is never among the three best (or within the margin) at any site under any of the nine metrics: the selection removes estimators that are dominated everywhere, it does not pick one winner.
